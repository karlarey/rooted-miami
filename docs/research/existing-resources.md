# Brain Gain: What Already Exists for Relocated Tech Workers in Miami

Research date: 2026-10-10. Persona: a software engineer who moved to Miami and is deciding whether to stay.
Status key: **Active** = dated activity in 2025-2026 found. **Likely active** = current listings, but nothing dated. **Unclear** = only pre-2024 evidence. **Dormant?** = signs it has stopped.

## TL;DR
- Miami has plenty of **event discovery**: the Refresh Miami calendar, Luma groups, and big annual conferences. It also has **student-pipeline workforce programs** (Miami Tech Works, Break Through Tech, CodePath, TalentBridge).
- Almost nothing is aimed at **retaining experienced engineers who already moved here**. The city's 2021 "relocation concierge" energy (Venture Miami) has faded. Its website did not resolve when we tried it in Oct 2026, and the newer mayor's stated focus is housing.
- The research says what keeps people in a place is **social offerings, openness, and aesthetics** (Knight/Gallup), not jobs alone. No Miami tool helps a newcomer go from "attended a meetup" to "has friends, a role, and roots."

### Evidence: do local grads stay?
- **About two-thirds of the region's graduates stay** (Richard Florida & Steven Pedigo, *Greater Miami Talent Scorecard*, 2019, FIU Miami Future Initiative). A 2016 Brookings LinkedIn analysis said more than 50%. https://iamstevenpedigo.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Final-Talent-Brief.pdf
- **FIU computing:** "of about 3,500 SCIS alumni, roughly one-third still live and work in South Florida" (undated FIU CEC article, so a weaker signal). https://cec.fiu.edu/?p=10295
- **Miami Tech Works Gap Report (June 2024):** employers import talent because local grads aren't retained. Software-developer retention must rise about 5% a year for 10 years to balance supply. MDC's Antonio Delgado: "the extremely bad job that we're doing in Miami retaining our graduates." https://refreshmiami.com/news/the-data-is-in-we-have-tech-talent-but-are-not-retaining-it-now-what/
- **Gensler survey (2024 fieldwork, reported Nov 2025-Feb 2026):** **51.8%** of childless 18-34-year-olds in Miami are likely to leave, 3rd highest of 27 metros. Reasons cited: cost of living, climate risk (about 29%), traffic, low-wage job mix. https://www.axios.com/2025/11/18/young-adults-likely-leave-city , https://www.wlrn.org/business/2026-01-29/i-cant-stay-in-miami-why-young-people-are-leaving-the-magic-city
- **Cost shock:** Miami ranks #1 nationally for homeowners-insurance burden (about 3.7% of home value per year). Renters insurance runs about 52% above the Florida average, flood coverage is a separate purchase, and hurricane deductibles are 2-5%. https://www.1800insurance.com/guides/miami-renters-insurance

### Evidence: does volunteering or mentoring build belonging and retention?
- **Knight/Gallup "Soul of the Community"** (43K people, 26 cities, 2008-2010): the top drivers of attachment to a place are **social offerings, openness (welcoming), and aesthetics**. These beat jobs and basic services. Attachment correlated with local GDP growth (correlational, not causal). Miami was one of the 26 cities. https://knightfoundation.org/sotc , https://news.gallup.com/poll/144476/social-offerings-openness-key-community-attachment.aspx
- **Small studies:** volunteering and place attachment reinforce each other.
  - Immigrant volunteers in Seoul: civic engagement leads to place attachment, which leads to more engagement (2021, qualitative). https://accesson.kr/kaser/v.31/1/168/30533?lang=en
  - Appalachian Trail volunteers: place attachment predicts continued service (n=115, 2018).
  - A 2026 preprint notes a "paucity of research" on newcomer volunteers and community. https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202608.1597
- **Bottom line:** there is a plausible, directionally supported mechanism (contribution leads to attachment leads to staying), but no strong causal evidence specifically for relocated knowledge workers. A hackathon team can cite Knight/Gallup as the anchor and frame the rest as a hypothesis.

## Gaps and opportunities

1. **No post-arrival retention layer.** Every program targets either attraction (2021 Venture Miami, events) or the student pipeline (FIU, MDC, Tech Works). Nothing follows up with an engineer at months 3, 6 or 12, which is when the "stay or go" decision happens.
2. **Event overload without continuity.** Refresh Miami and Luma list dozens of events, but they are one-off and VC/founder-heavy. Nothing helps someone return to the *same* small group repeatedly, which is how acquaintances become friends. hello_miami is the closest, but it is builder-only.
3. **Friends outside work.** No tool matches tech newcomers by neighborhood, life stage (kids, dogs, partner), language, or hobbies (sailing, run clubs, dominoes, salsa). Generic apps (Bumble BFF, Timeleft) aren't Miami-tuned or tech-aware.
4. **Mentoring is hard for a newcomer to join.**
   - FIU and Break Through Tech plug in at the employer level.
   - UM Cane2Cane requires being a UM alum.
   - MDC programs are ad hoc. M-DCPS volunteering is generic and background-check-heavy.
   - National programs (CodePath, Black Girls CODE) aren't Miami-routed.
   - **No single place says "here are 5 local student mentoring slots matching your stack and schedule, 2 hrs/month, commit now."** This directly targets the "openness/contribution" side of attachment *and* the local-grad retention problem (a two-sided win).
5. **Neighborhood fit beyond real-estate marketing.** Existing guides are static or broker-biased. No tool combines commute to tech hubs (Wynwood, Brickell, Coral Gables), flood zone and insurance cost, where other engineers live, and nearby community events.
6. **Cost and insurance surprises.** Nothing warns newcomers early about renters/flood insurance, hurricane deductibles, car insurance, or how Miami tech pay compares to cost of living. These show up as "Miami is more expensive than I thought" and feed the 51.8% intent-to-leave figure.
7. **Next-job anxiety.** The Refresh Miami job board exists, but there is no "if my remote job ends, can I find a local role?" view: no local company map by stack, no warm intros, and no senior peer network. Pay below national levels (CBRE) makes this worse.
8. **The civic lane has gone quiet.** Code for Miami-style civic tech appears dormant, and Venture Miami's city-run concierge appears gone. That leaves a gap for a lightweight, community-run "welcome wagon" for technologists, possibly partnering with Refresh Miami (distribution) and Talent Bridge Miami (which is already procuring a talent portal).

**Suggested framing for the team:** a "roots score" or onboarding companion for the first 12 months. It would include a recurring small-group cohort, a matched 2-hr/month student mentoring slot (FIU, MDC, Code/Art), a neighborhood and insurance reality check, and a local job-safety-net view. It should be built *on top of* Refresh Miami's calendar and job board, not as a replacement.

### Caveats
- Several "status" calls are inferred from listings, not confirmed with organizers. In particular, verify Venture Miami, The LAB Miami, Code for Miami, PyMiami, and Miami Hack Week before citing them in a pitch.
- The Venture Miami website failing to resolve could be a transient problem.
